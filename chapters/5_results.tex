\section{Results}

\subsection{Patient characteristics for treated patients and matched controls}

Table~\ref{tab:matching} shows the characteristics of treated patients and their matched controls (used for target trail emulation) in each treatment group. Patients who received MT, with or without IVT, had more severe strokes and less pre-stroke disability than those who received IVT alone, and those who received MT alone were far more likely to have AF and to be taking anticoagulants for AF. Matching produced closely balanced groups, and at most 2.6\% of any treatment group could not be matched. Characteristics of all patients before matching are given in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Effect of each treatment strategy}

Table~\ref{tab:effects} summarises the estimated effect of each strategy by target trial emulation, the S-learner and the causal forest. Figures of these estimates for each method are given in the Supplementary Material. Every strategy was associated with a significantly higher chance of a good outcome. All three methods found that early combined IVT and MT gave the largest benefit: 46.5\% of treated patients had a good outcome against 23.5\% of matched controls, the S-learner log odds ratio was 1.35 (odds ratio 3.9), and the causal forest estimated an absolute gain of 18.8 percentage points. Early MT alone gave the next largest benefit by matching and the S-learner, followed by early IVT alone.

Later treatment gave less benefit. On the log odds scale, late IVT alone and late MT alone gave about half to three quarters of the benefit of their early counterparts, and early IVT followed by late MT gave little more than early IVT alone (log odds ratio 0.73 against 0.71). Only 141 patients received both treatments late, and their estimates were imprecise.

Having established that all estimates of treatment effect were similar, we used the S-learner to analyse heterogeneity of treatment effect further.

\subsection{Effect of time to treatment}

Figure~\ref{fig:main_s_learner_matrix} shows the benefit estimated by the S-learner fitted to continuous times to IVT and MT, grouped into one-hour intervals. The benefit of IVT alone and of MT alone declined over the six hours after onset. A linear fit to the benefit of IVT alone gave a log odds ratio of 1.19 (95\% CI 1.17--1.21) extrapolated to the time of onset, falling to zero at 405 minutes (95\% CI 395--415). For MT alone, the corresponding values were 1.54 (95\% CI 1.47--1.62) and 596 minutes (95\% CI 572--621). These correspond to losses of about 0.18 and 0.16 log odds units per hour of delay, so that each hour multiplied the odds ratio for a good outcome by about 0.84 for IVT and 0.86 for MT (reducing the improved odds each hour).

The two treatment times interacted. For a fixed time to MT, benefit was greater the earlier IVT had been given: for MT at 240--300 minutes, the log odds ratio was 0.84 without IVT, 0.81--0.82 with IVT at 180--300 minutes, 0.99 with IVT at 120--180 minutes, and 1.29 with IVT at 60--120 minutes. For a fixed time to IVT, benefit fell the later MT followed and converged on that of IVT alone: for IVT at 60--120 minutes, the log odds ratio was 1.70 with MT at 120--180 minutes, 1.37 at 180--240 minutes, 1.29 at 240--300 minutes and 1.11 at 300--360 minutes, against 0.98 for IVT alone. IVT given at 180--240 minutes added nothing to MT performed in the same or the following hour, but was associated with an additional 0.2--0.3 log odds units when MT took place at 300--480 minutes. The benefit of early combined treatment (log odds ratio 1.35) was less than the sum of the benefits of early IVT alone and early MT alone (0.71 and 1.19).

\subsection{Characteristics associated with greater benefit}

A secondary model predicted the estimated benefit of treatment in treated patients with an $R^2$ of 0.789 (Figure~\ref{fig:main_s_learner_benefit_shap_mrs_2}). The features most associated with benefit were the stroke team attended, stroke severity, pre-stroke disability, and the type and timing of treatment. Benefit increased with stroke severity and decreased with pre-stroke disability (reversing with the highest pre-stroke disability).
