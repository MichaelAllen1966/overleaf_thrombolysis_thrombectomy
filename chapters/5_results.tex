Those receiving IVT, with or without MT, had significantly lower rates of atrial fibrillation (AF) diagnosis and of anticoagulant use for AF. Other characteristics were similar between groups.

Figure \ref{fig:main_target_trial_mrs_2} shows the differences between control and treated groups for each treatment group. Early (treatment given $\leq$ 270 minutes after stroke onset) combined IVT and MT had the greatest estimated benefit, followed by early IVT alone. Early MT alone had a significant benefit, but less than that of combined treatment or IVT alone. Late IVT alone ($>$ 270 minutes after stroke onset) had a reduced benefit, but late MT, with or without IVT, appeared to have little benefit.

\subsection{S-Learner}

Figure \ref{fig:main_s_learner_benefit} shows the estimated treatment effect for each treatment group, as estimated by the S-Learner. Early (treatment given $\leq$ 270 minutes after stroke onset) combined IVT and MT had the greatest estimated benefit, followed by early IVT alone. Early MT alone had a significant benefit, but less than that of combined treatment or IVT alone. Late IVT alone ($>$ 270 minutes after stroke onset) had a reduced benefit. Late MT, with or without IVT, had a further reduced benefit (although estimates for late IVT with late MT were highly variable owing to the small sample size).

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{images/treatment_effects/xgboost_counterfactual_cate_log_odds_estimates_2.png}
    \caption{Estimated counterfactual treatment benefit across reperfusion-treatment timing strategies, derived from the XGBoost S-learner model, comparing the odds of being discharged with mRS 0--2 with and without treatment. Points represent the mean estimated individual treatment effect for each treatment-timing group, expressed as the counterfactual log odds ratio for being discharged with mRS 0--2 relative to no reperfusion treatment; horizontal error bars show 95\% confidence intervals. Treatment groups are defined by receipt of IVT and/or MT, with early treatment defined as onset-to-treatment time $\leq$270 minutes and late treatment as $>$270 minutes. Sample sizes are shown alongside group labels. Values above zero indicate a model-predicted increase in the odds of being discharged with mRS 0--2 under the observed treatment strategy compared with no treatment.}
    \label{fig:main_s_learner_benefit}
\end{figure}

Figure \ref{fig:main_s_learner_matrix} shows results from the S-Learner fitted to continuous times to IVT and MT, with times then grouped into one-hour intervals. IVT alone and MT alone both showed declining benefit over the six hours after stroke onset. The magnitude of benefit was similar for each treatment at comparable times. Combined treatment showed some benefit over either treatment alone.

When a linear regression model was fitted to the benefit of IVT alone against time to IVT, the maximum log-odds benefit (of being discharged with mRS 0--2), extrapolated back to the time of stroke onset, was 1.19 (95\% CI 1.17--1.21). The time of no effect after stroke onset was 405 min (95\% CI 395--415 min). For MT alone, the maximum log-odds benefit extrapolated back to the time of stroke onset was 1.54 (95\% CI 1.47--1.62), and the time of no effect was 596 min (95\% CI 572--621 min).

After estimating treatment effects, we fitted an XGBoost regression model to understand what influences the benefit of treatment. This model predicted estimated benefit with an $R^2$ of 0.789. Figure \ref{fig:main_s_learner_benefit_shap_mrs_2} shows the SHAP values for that model, in order of their overall influence on treatment benefit. The most influential features affecting the estimated benefit of treatment (in treated patients only) were the stroke team attended, stroke severity, prior disability, and the type and time of treatment.

\subsection{Causal Forest}

Figure \ref{fig:main_causal_forest_mrs_2} shows the estimated treatment effect for each treatment group, as estimated by the Causal Forest. Early (treatment given $\leq$ 270 minutes after stroke onset) combined IVT and MT had the greatest estimated benefit, followed by early IVT alone. Early MT alone had a significant benefit, but less than that of combined treatment or IVT alone. Late IVT alone ($>$ 270 minutes after stroke onset) had a reduced benefit. Late MT, with or without IVT, had a further reduced benefit (although estimates for late IVT with late MT were highly variable owing to the small sample size).
